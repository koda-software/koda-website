## Invitation and sign-in

You start with an invitation that arrives at your company e-mail address. The invitation leads to setting up access, and from then on that address is the user's identity in the system.

**The account is personal.** It is not a departmental or shared access. Every action you take is signed with your first and last name: the team is meant to know who entered what.

## Five minutes to start

Before you begin working, go through a short list. It saves a lot of confusion in the first week.

1. **Complete your profile.** Your first and last name are visible next to the records and tasks you enter. Check your e-mail address too, because notifications go there.
2. **Set the language.** It changes the interface labels; it does not change data entered by the team.
3. **Check which company you are in.** If the organization runs several companies, this is the most important habit to start with.
4. **Look at your tasks.** This is usually the first thing you open every day.
5. **Browse the menu without clicking anything hastily.** It is worth seeing first what is where.

> **Look around before you start entering data.** The first impulse is usually "I'll create a record and see what happens." It is better to first look at a few existing records in the area you are to work in. You will see how the team describes data and avoid entering something in a different style from the rest.

## Sources of tasks and notifications

Work reaches the user from two sources.

| Source | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Tasks | a matter directed to the user by a person or a process |
| Notifications | something happened that you should know about |

A task requires action and waits until you complete it. A notification usually only informs. This difference matters: **the task list is work to be done, and notifications are information**.

## The first record

When it comes to entering something of your own, remember three things.

- **Fill in the descriptive fields sensibly.** A record's name is not the place for a shorthand that only you understand. Six months from now someone will be searching for it.
- **Check the company before saving**, if you work across several.
- **Do not be afraid to save.** A record can be completed later, and the system records the change history, so nothing is lost for good.

> **Example.** Instead of naming a record "New 2", name it the way you would in a conversation with a colleague: "Service contract Kowalski, March". This is one of those things that cost five seconds and save the team hours of searching.

## Practices not recommended at the start

- **Do not create "trial" records in production.** They stay in the lists and someone later counts them in reports. If you want to practice, ask the administrator to point out a place.
- **Do not work under someone else's account**, even when someone suggests "because it's faster".
- **Do not correct other people's records without agreement**, until you know how the team works.

## No access to an area

This is the most common question in the first week, and it is usually not a fault. The reason is often the access scope for a given role, a different company, or an area not enabled in the company. Ask the administrator, stating specifically what you are looking for and why.
