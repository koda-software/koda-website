## A process as configuration

The flow of a matter, the approvals, the order of stages, all of this in Opero is a **setting, not a program to be written**. That is why a new process or a correction of an existing one arises in days, not months, and does not require a system update.

One thing matters: describing well how a matter is to flow. The more specific, the fewer rounds of correction.

## Describing the need

The administrator will build a process faster with this information.

- **Stages.** The states a matter passes through, in order. For example: submitted, to approve, approved, completed.
- **Decisions.** Where someone approves or rejects something and what happens then. A rejection usually turns a matter back; it is worth saying where to.
- **Who does what.** At each stage: who is to perform the task and who may push the matter on.
- **Conditions.** Whether there are things without which one may not move on, for example a missing amount or a missing attachment.
- **Endings.** How a matter may end. Often there is more than one ending, for example completed and rejected.

> **Example.** Instead of "I want an invoice flow", write: an invoice comes in as To check (accountant), after checking it goes To approve (manager), the manager approves or rejects with a comment, and after approval the invoice is Approved. That is enough to build a process without further questions.

## Effects of a change

- **A change comes in wholesale and at once**, after the administrator publishes it. Until then the old version is in force.
- **Matters already started will finish under the old rules.** The new flow works for matters started after the change. This is intended, so as not to overturn matters in progress.
- **Data does not disappear.** Changing the flow does not touch the records' contents or the history.

## Incorrect process behavior

Report a specific matter and the course that did not match expectations: the record number, what you did, what you expected, what happened. The administrator will reconstruct that matter's course, and it usually turns out to be one of two things: either a case the flow does not cover came up, or the matter went exactly as arranged, only the arrangement needs a correction.
