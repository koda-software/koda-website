## The scope of profile data

A profile looks like a private matter, but in practice it is an **element of teamwork**. From it the system takes the data others see: at an assigned task, at a comment, at a record you created.

Three things in the profile affect others more than it seems.

- **First and last name.** They appear everywhere the author is shown. An abbreviation or a nickname makes work harder for people looking for whom to talk to about a matter.
- **E-mail address.** Notifications from the system go to it. An outdated address means you stop getting information about tasks.
- **Phone.** It is sometimes the only way to reach you in an urgent matter.

## The working language

In the profile you set the language you work in. The change concerns the **interface**: the names of buttons, the menu, the system's messages.

It does not change the data entered by the team. A record described in Polish stays in Polish regardless of the language you set, unless someone has prepared a translation for it. This distinction is worth knowing, because it is sometimes a source of misunderstandings in international teams.

## Account security

An account in Opero gives access to the company's data, so treat it like access to company mail.

- **Do not share the account.** Working under someone else's account means the records' history attributes changes to the wrong person, and that spoils work for everyone.
- **Enable extra sign-in confirmation**, if the organization uses it. It is a few seconds at sign-in and a real difference in an attempt to take over the account.
- **Report a suspicious sign-in** to the administrator, instead of waiting for it to "sort itself out".

> **An account is not a shared mailbox.** An account created "for the department" is the wrong solution: the system stops showing who actually made a change, and at the first dispute about data there is no way to reconstruct it.

## A user's absence

Leave or a longer absence require one action: **assigned tasks do not disappear**. They wait until you return, or they block a matter for someone else.

Before a longer absence, review your tasks and arrange with your manager who will take them over. Reassigning a task is an ordinary operation, but someone has to know about it in advance.

> **Example.** A leave request awaits approval from a person who is themselves on leave. The matter stands until their return, because no one else has this task on their list. That is why a substitution is arranged before the trip.

## A change of position or departure

When you change your scope of duties, permissions do not change automatically. Ask the administrator to adjust the access: it is usually a matter of adding one area and removing another.

On leaving the company, the account is deactivated, but **the entries and history remain in the system**. This is normal and needed: documents must stay complete and the history consistent.
