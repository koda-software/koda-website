## Changes come in deliberately

Opero's configuration does not change by accident. The administrator works on a **draft version** no one but them sees, and a change becomes visible only at the moment of **publication**. Thanks to this, it does not happen that a field disappears while you are filling in a form because someone is just moving it.

A practical consequence: between requesting a change and its appearing in the system there is a moment when the change already exists but does not yet work. This is a normal stage of the work, not a forgotten request.

## What to do after a change is deployed

If you had the system open at the moment of publication, refresh the page. A screen built before the change still shows the previous version of the layout.

A configuration change **does not touch data**. Moving a field on a form, adding a section, or changing a name does not change the records' contents.

## Problems after a version change

Report it to the administrator as specifically as possible: what you did, what you expected, and what happened. Previous versions of the layouts are kept, so returning to the state before the change is usually quick.

> **Example.** After a change to the ticket form, two fields swapped places and the team started getting confused. A report and the publication of a corrected version the same day were enough, without waiting for a system update.

## What publication does not cover

- **Permissions.** A new layout does not grant access to data that was not visible before.
- **Records' history.** Saved values and history entries stay untouched.
- **Numbering.** Assigned numbers remain as they were.
