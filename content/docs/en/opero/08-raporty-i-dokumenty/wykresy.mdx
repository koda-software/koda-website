## What a chart is for

A chart is a report shown as an image instead of numbers. The human eye catches a trend on a line or the difference in bar heights faster than the same information in a table full of figures. When the **general picture** matters (rising or falling, who is bigger, what share), a chart says it at a single glance.

A table and a chart often show the same data. You choose the table when you want an exact number, the chart when you want to see a relationship.

## Kinds of charts and what they suit

| Chart | Best for |
| --- | --- |
| Bar | comparing sizes between categories, for example regions' sales |
| Line | change over time, a trend month by month |
| Area | a trend with the size under the line emphasized |
| Pie and donut | the share of parts in the whole, for example the split of tickets by type |
| Scatter | the relationship between two values |

The administrator matches the kind to the question. A trend over time is usually a line, shares a pie, a comparison of categories bars.

> **Example.** A ticket report shows a line chart with the number of tickets in successive months. At a glance you see the seasonal spike over the holidays, which in a table you would have to look for number by number.

## Reading a chart

- **The horizontal axis** is usually the breakdown, for example months or categories.
- **The vertical axis** is the value: an amount, a count, an average.
- **Series**, that is, different colors of bars or lines, are the compared groups, for example several regions at once. The legend says what is what.

## A chart and a table are one report

Many reports let you switch between the chart and the table of the same summary. You look at the chart to see a trend, and switch to the table when you need the exact number from a specific point. It is the same computed content in two forms.

## What to keep in mind

- **A chart computes from the available data range**, just like a table. Different permissions or a different company mean a different picture.
- **A chart does not edit data.** It shows the state computed from records; changes are made on the forms, and the chart refreshes the next time it is opened.
- **Filters work on the chart too.** Narrowing to one year or department changes the picture without touching the report itself.
