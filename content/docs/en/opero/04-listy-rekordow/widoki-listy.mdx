## One set of data, three ways to show it

A record list does not have to look like a table. The same set of matters can be displayed **as a list, as a kanban board, or as a calendar**. These are not three different places in the system nor three copies of the data: they are three layouts of the same list.

The choice of layout depends on the configuration of the given list or custom list. Some places have one fixed layout; in others you can switch.

## Uses of the individual layouts

| Layout | Shows | Suits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| List | rows with columns of selected fields | finding a specific matter, comparing, exporting |
| Kanban | cards laid out in columns | assessing where the work stands and what is piling up |
| Calendar | records placed on days | work where the date matters: visits, deadlines, a schedule |

A practical rule: **use the list when you look for one matter, and kanban or the calendar when you want to see the whole**. Kanban answers the question "where do we stand"; the calendar answers "what and when".

> **Example.** The same ticket list: on the list you filter by client and find a specific ticket from a month ago. On the kanban you see that twenty tickets await verification and nothing is in delivery. On the calendar you check how many visits fall on Thursday.

## Elements common to all layouts

This is the most important thing in this topic, because it saves misunderstandings.

- **The data is the same.** Changing the layout creates and deletes nothing. A record added on the kanban you will see on the list and vice versa.
- **The permissions are the same.** A layout shows nothing you do not have access to. If you do not see something on the list, it will not appear on the calendar either.
- **Filters usually work the same.** Narrowing the set concerns the data, not the way it is drawn.

> **An empty calendar is usually a missing date, not missing records.** The calendar places a record on a day based on a date field. A record without a date filled in will not be placed on the calendar, even though it appears normally on the list. When the calendar looks empty, check it on the list first.

## Limitations of a layout

A layout is a way of presenting, so it **does not affect what may be done with a matter**. If a card cannot be moved to another column on the board, the reason lies in the process or in the permissions, not in the fact that you are looking at a kanban instead of a list.

The same works the other way around: if a given list has no calendar view, that is a configuration decision, not a fault.
