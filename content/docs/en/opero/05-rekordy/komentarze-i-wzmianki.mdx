## A conversation at the record

A comment is a note or a message pinned directly to a record: to a ticket, a contract, a project. Instead of writing about a matter in an e-mail or a chat, where the content is lost, you write **at the matter itself**. Thanks to this, anyone who opens the record sees its whole conversation history in one place.

Comments have a fixed place: the **Comments tab in the form's side panel**. They are ordered in time: the newest join the thread and the older ones remain, so you can see what was agreed and when.

## A mention calls a specific person

If you want a specific person to see a comment, **tag it with a mention** by typing `@` and choosing the person. The tagged person will get a notification and go straight to that record. This is the difference between "I wrote something, maybe someone will read it" and "I asked a specific person to respond".

- A mention **notifies** the tagged person, provided they belong to this company.
- You can tag a person or a role, so you call "the matter owner" without having to know who it currently is.
- A mention grants no access. If someone has no right to see a record, tagging alone will not open it for them.

> **Example.** You finish a quote and want your supervisor's approval. In a comment you write "@Anna Nowak please approve, the amount and scope agreed with the client". Anna gets a notification, opens the record, and sees exactly what you are asking for.

## Uses of comments

- **Agreements**: what was agreed, why such a decision was made.
- **Requests**: asking someone for a step, with a mention.
- **Context for the next person**: a note that will save questions when someone else takes over the matter.

## Practices not recommended in comments

- **Do not keep in a comment data that is a field.** A deadline, an amount, a status belong to the record's fields, because that is where they are filtered and counted. A comment is a conversation, not a place for data.
- **Do not count on a comment to replace a task.** If something is to happen in a process, a mention will remind, but it is the task in the process that ensures the step is done.

## Permanence

Comments stay at the record permanently and are part of its history. So write in a way that makes a note understandable half a year from now to someone who did not take part in the conversation.
