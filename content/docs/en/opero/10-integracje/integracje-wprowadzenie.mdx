## What a systems integration means

An integration is a **connection between Opero and another program** through which data passes between them without manual retyping. An order from a shop can create a matter in Opero by itself, and data from invoices can go to accounting without sending a spreadsheet by e-mail.

An integration is usually **invisible** to the user. It shows itself in something appearing by itself or in your not having to enter something a second time in another place.

## How to tell that data came from outside

- **A record no one created by hand.** A matter appears in the list because another system created it.
- **A "system" author in the history.** At a change there is a mark of an automation or a connection, not a first and last name. This is intended behavior.
- **Fields no one filled in.** The data came from outside together with the record.

> **Example.** A client places an order in an online shop. In Opero a new matter appears with the client's data and the order items, even though no one on the team entered anything. You handle the delivery, not the retyping.

## What to do with wrong data from an integration

Integrations can be sensitive to changes on the other side. If you see that something came in incomplete or strange:

- **Do not fix it yourself on every occurrence.** If the same error returns on every record, the problem is in the connection, not in this one case.
- **Report it to the administrator with specifics**: which record, when it appeared, what is wrong. The administrator will read from the system what exactly came from outside.
- **Do not create a record by hand "just in case"**, if you expect it to come from the integration. You will duplicate the matter.

## Limitations of an integration

An integration moves data, but **does not decide for you**. What is to happen with a brought-in matter is still described by a process in Opero: who verifies it, who approves it, when it is closed. A connection with another system does not replace the established flow.
